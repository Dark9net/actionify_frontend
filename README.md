## Landing page sections (`src/components/`)

1. **Hero** (`Hero.jsx`): the opening frame with the title behind the cut-out, then a pinned sequence of full-screen event frames that wipe in on scroll. The frames follow an eased copy of the scroll position (`FOLLOW` in `Hero.jsx`), so the wheel glides rather than steps; each picture settles from a zoom and rises inside its frame as it is uncovered, while the frame underneath drifts up and darkens. After the last frame the hero melts into white (`--melt` in `hero.css`) and the film grain fades with it, so it meets the LENS section without a seam.
2. **Booking / LENS** (`Booking.jsx`): the word splits around a pill-shaped showreel. The section pins to the screen and the scroll drives the reveal: the hairline opens to a slit, a circle, then the full pill, with the paper, the caption and the button following; scrolling back closes it again. The track is 2.6 screens tall (`.bk-track` in `booking.css`); `LEAD` and `SPAN` at the top of `Booking.jsx` set where the reveal starts and how much of the scroll it takes. Links to `#book` land on the finished frame. **Book a Photographer** replays that effect and, when it has finished, opens the booking form in a sheet from the right (`BookingSheet.jsx`).
3. **Statement** (`Statement.jsx`): the slogan, letter by letter, with two round photos; pointed at, a photo stretches sideways into a capsule (`.pl:hover` in `statement.css`, sized so both lines still fit on their rows). Under it, a 35-word statement that lights up word by word as it is read (the `word-highlight` moment, below). From here down, every section sits on the one paper ground (`--paper`).
4. **Services** (`Services.jsx`): an editorial index of the events covered, one large serif row each, under a short introduction (`services.intro`). Pointing at a row turns its name rust from left to right, draws a rust rule under it, fills its arrow and steps the other rows back; a photograph of that kind of event trails the pointer, each one uncovered in turn as you move down the list. Focusing a row with the keyboard pins the photograph beside it. Under 900px wide each row carries its own thumbnail instead. Each row opens Our Work filtered to that kind of event.
5. **Recent work** (`WorkPreview.jsx`): five frames as a strip of tall panels that wipe in one after another. The open panel (the first at rest, then the one pointed at or focused) widens to show its whole frame, its kind of event and title; the closed ones are slices with their number and kind on the edge. On phones and touch screens they sit in a grid, labelled at rest. Each opens Our Work at its kind of event.
6. **How hiring works** (`Facts.jsx`): title, a "Watch the showreel" badge (it leads to the LENS section's showreel), copy, facts table, a booking button, and a portrait that develops like a print in the tray as it comes on screen (the `picture-develops` moment, below).
7. **Footer** (`Footer.jsx`, `footer.css`): on every page, exactly as tall as its 1440 × 672 layout (no empty band on tall screens; on phones, as tall as its content). A dusk-meadow video in its own colours, under a paper wash at the top only (behind the columns); at its foot nothing but the video and "actionify" set large in the site's light amber (`--rust-light`), its letters rising in one after another as the name scrolls into view and leaving one after another, last letter first, as you scroll back up (the last letter lands just as the page runs out), and four columns: the studio (with a frosted-glass "Book a photographer" button), pages, services (each glides to its photos on Our Work), contact (the email, then Instagram and Facebook as round glass icon buttons; their addresses are in `studio.social`). The video loads only as the footer nears the screen and stays still under reduced motion. Its title, credit line and video address are in `footer` in `content.js`.

Photograph corners come from `--r-img` and `--r-sm` in `base.css`; hover states share the `--ease-ui` curve.

## Shared parts

- **Header** (`SiteHeader.jsx`, `header.css`): the logo mark (`public/actionify-mark.svg`, also the favicon) and name on the left, the pages from `pages` in the content file centred on a frosted capsule, **Book a photographer** on the right. Pointing at a link brings up a paper pill that slides from link to link; the current page carries a short rust bar; the logo tilts as a glint crosses it; once the visitor has scrolled back up, a rust hairline along the top edge of the screen shows how far down the page they are. The header has no background of its own: the logo sits on a small frosted pill and the links on their capsule.
- **Book a photographer** (`BookButton.jsx`, `book-button.css`): a pane of frosted glass (the page blurred behind a pale tint, a bright top edge, a soft sheen) with ink text and an ink arrow disc. A thin amber light runs round its border, one lap every 3.5 s. Pointing at it (or focusing it) fans out its three small prints, tints the glass warm and turns the disc rust, swaps the arrow and lifts it 1px; pressing it presses it in. The prints deal in once as the page loads; they are `bookPrints` in the content file. Under reduced motion the light, the fan and the deal are all off. Scrolling down, everything but the logo steps away (the logo stays on a small frosted pill); scrolling up brings the whole header back. Keyboard focus inside it, an open menu, or a visitor who asks for reduced motion keeps it in view. Under 1100px wide the six links move into the menu; under 640px the booking button does too.
- **"Book a photographer" buttons** (`.glass-btn` in `site.css`): every one is a pane of frosted glass like the header's: the LENS section on Home, the facts block, the closing band on each page, the Contact Us side panel, the footer, and the menu's booking card (`.glass-btn--dark`, a smoky pane on its photograph). Hover sweeps a band of light across the pane, brightens its rim, lifts it 2px and deepens its shadow; press scales it to .97. Under reduced motion only the colours change.
- **Accounts** (`src/lib/auth.js`): the site has no accounts of its own. Set `VITE_AUTH_URL` in a `.env` file to the gallery platform's API and sign-in, sign-up, password reset and gallery codes post JSON to `<VITE_AUTH_URL>/sign-in`, `/sign-up`, `/reset` and `/open-code`. Without it, `npm run dev` (or a build with `VITE_AUTH_DEMO=true`) pretends every request worked, so each screen can be seen and a gallery code opens the sample gallery; the built site says sign-in is not open yet and points to the gallery link or QR code.
- **Links to a section** (`ScrollManager.jsx`): a link with an anchor, such as the services links (`/our-work?show=concerts#gallery`, from the home page's services index, the work panels and the footer) or `/our-details#kit`, opens the page at its top and glides down to that section; on the same page it glides from where you are. Under reduced motion it jumps. Plain links start at the top; back and forward, and the Our Work filters, leave the scroll alone.
- **Back to top** (`BackToTop.jsx`, `back-to-top.css`): a round ink button at the bottom right, on every page long enough to need it. It comes in only in the last 40% of a screen before the end and leaves again as you scroll back up; its light-amber ring draws round over that stretch and closes at the very bottom. It sits just above the footer's big wordmark, never on its letters. Hover lifts it and turns it rust, the arrow runs up and a small "Top" tab slides out; a click glides to the top and moves keyboard focus to the start of the page. Under reduced motion it fades and the page jumps.
- **Menu sheet** (`Sidebar.jsx`, `overlays.css`): opens from the right, from the header's menu button on tablets and phones. The brand and a round close button; one row per page with its number, a short line and a small photograph (`hint` and `thumb` in `pages` in the content file), the current page on a raised panel with its name in rust; a booking card on a photograph (`menuCard`) with the delivery time and the button; the slogan, the email and the social links. The rows, the card and the foot deal in as it opens; under reduced motion it simply appears. Esc, a tap outside or the close button closes it.
- **Moments** (`hooks/useMoment.js`): the two scroll and entrance effects from the taste-skill kit. Its loader and the two moments live unchanged in `public/assets/moments/`; don't edit those files, replace them from the skill instead. Each moment is marked in the markup with `data-moment="<id>"` and mounted per element by `useMoment()`, since the kit's own page scan runs only once and this app swaps pages without reloading. Both show their finished state under reduced motion.
- **In-page links that glide** (`lib/scroll.js`): the page has no global smooth scrolling, so keyboard focus jumps straight to what it lands on; the two links to `#book` glide there themselves.
- **The exploded camera** (`Kit.jsx`, `KitBand.jsx`, `kit.css`): `public/assets/exploded-dslr-cut.webp`, the camera with the paper and its floor shadow keyed out (the original is kept in `design-src/`, outside the published files). On Our Details the section pins for about two screens of scroll: the body is in place, then the lens, top plates, boards, back and battery each slide in from just outside their place (six copies of the one image, each clipped to its parts by `PARTS` in `Kit.jsx`), and five numbered callouts draw out. The callouts' text and positions are `kit.callouts` in the content file. Under reduced motion it is shown laid out.
- **Scroll progress** (`hooks/useScrollProgress.js`): writes how far an element has scrolled as `--p` for CSS to read; the camera, the banner, the steps and the day use it. Simpler entrances use the `.sr` class (`pages.css`), driven by the browser's scroll timeline where it has one and shown in place otherwise.
- **Booking form** (`BookingForm.jsx`): one form, used in the booking sheet and on Hire Us.
- **Sheets and the photo viewer** are native `<dialog>` elements driven by `hooks/useDialog.js`, so Esc, the focus trap and returning focus come from the browser.

**Uploads** (`lib/uploads.js`, `lib/r2.js`, `UploadTray.jsx`). Three photographs go up at a time, whichever page is open. Each one is queued → uploading (progress, speed) → done; a dropped connection is retried by itself twice (after 1.5 s and 4 s, "Retrying in 3 s"), then marked failed with the reason and a Retry button. The tray at the bottom right shows the whole batch (photos done, megabytes sent, speed, time left, a ring for the progress), each photo with Stop or Retry, and Retry all failed, Stop all, Clear finished. A finished batch brings a toast ("16 photos uploaded", Open event; or "3 uploads failed", Retry). Closing the tab mid-upload asks first. Keys are `events/<event>/<category>/<file>`; a name already used gets `-2`. Renaming an event or a category changes only the name shown: nothing on R2 moves.

**Toasts** (`lib/toast.js`, `Toaster.jsx`): success, error or info, with an action (Retry, Undo, Open event); they pause while pointed at, errors stay longer and are read out at once.

**Connecting Cloudflare R2.** The browser never holds the bucket's keys. Put these in a `.env` file:

```bash
VITE_R2_SIGN_URL=https://studio-api.example/r2/sign   # POST { key, contentType, size } → { url, headers? }
VITE_R2_PUBLIC_URL=https://photos.example             # optional: where the bucket is served from
VITE_R2_BUCKET=actionify-galleries                   # shown on the Storage page
```

The signing endpoint (a Cloudflare Worker is the natural home) checks that the person is signed in to the studio and returns a presigned R2 `PUT` URL for that key (the S3 API with `aws4fetch`, or a Worker route that writes the request body to the bucket binding). The bucket needs a CORS rule allowing `PUT` from the panel's address. Without `VITE_R2_SIGN_URL` the panel runs on **demo storage**: uploads are timed as if they went to R2, about 1 in 14 drops part-way so retry can be seen (`VITE_R2_DEMO_FAIL=0` for never), previews are kept in this browser (IndexedDB), and nothing leaves the device.

**Before it goes live:**

- **Sign-in.** The panel has no sign-in of its own yet: put `/photographer` behind the studio's accounts (and have the signing endpoint check them) before the site is public.
- **Records.** Bookings, clients, payments, the team, access codes and storage come from the studio's API. `lib/store.js` (localStorage, starting empty) keeps only what the server sent for events and their codes; there is no sample data.
- **Removing photos** takes them out of the gallery here; deleting the files from R2 needs the API too.
- **Status colours** (pending amber, on site brick, completed teal) were checked for colour-blind readers with the dataviz palette validator; amber is under 3:1 on the panes, so every chart part in it also carries its label and number.

## Make it yours

Everything you are likely to change is in **`src/data/content.js`**: brand name, all texts, slogans, quotes, the page list, every image and video URL. To use your own photographs, put them in `public/` and reference them as `"/wedding-01.jpg"`.

Before going live:

- **Contact details.** `studio` in `content.js` holds placeholders (`hello@actionify.example`, generic Instagram and Facebook links). Put in your own. `phone` and `location` appear on the site as soon as they are filled in.
- **Where the forms send.** There is no server behind the site, so both forms hand their details to the visitor's email app as a ready-written message to `studio.email`. To collect requests directly, change the one function in `src/lib/enquiry.js` to post to your form service.
- **The photographers.** `details.team` in `content.js` lists four placeholder photographers (names, focus, kit) on stand-in photographs. Replace them with your own team.
- **Photographs.** All photographs are samples from Unsplash, and the hero capture details are sample captions. Replace them with your own work; the footer's "Sample photographs from Unsplash" line can then go (`Footer.jsx`).
- **Hosting.** The pages use real addresses (`/our-work`), so the host must serve `index.html` for any address (most static hosts call this an SPA fallback or rewrite).

Notes:

- A hero frame with `orientation: 'portrait'` is shown whole on a blurred copy of itself on wide screens and fills the screen on phones.
- `STEP` at the top of `Hero.jsx` sets how much scrolling each frame takes (in screens).
- In the Our Work list, `'l'` is a landscape tile and `'p'` a portrait tile.
